# Theme brainstorm: "Director's Cut"

**Theme status:** "Director's Cut" per another participant's devlog. Not yet confirmed from the official jam page.
Theme is one of seven judging criteria (scored 1–5), so it should be woven into mechanics, not just the art.

## Core reframe (strongest)

**Your soul bits are the scenes that were cut from your life.**
The corporation is a studio that *edited you*: it cut the parts of you that didn't serve the production. The Astral Zone is the cutting room floor. Recovering soul bits = restoring the deleted scenes. Reaching Source = seeing the uncut film: the director's cut of your own life.

## Mechanic ideas

1. **"CUT!" = rewind on capture.** When the stalker catches you, a voice calls "Cut. Again." A slate snaps: `TAKE 4`. You're back at your desk. The take number climbs all run.
2. **Script pages as clues.** You find pages of a screenplay with stage directions about *you*: "EMPLOYEE 4471 hesitates at the door." Sometimes they predict what you're about to do. They carry the hidden Key Lock targets. Agents plant fake pages (false bits).
3. **NPCs are actors.** Coworkers are playing roles. Malware agents are the ones who break character: a missed mark, a mouthed line, a glance at an invisible camera.
4. **Letterbox = being directed.** As Fear rises, black cinema bars close in from top and bottom, with film grain and flicker. The more cinematic it looks, the less control you have. Soul Sight pushes the bars back.
5. **Astral logic = editing reality.** Telekinesis is "moving props." Float is "wire work." Stretch: a short **scrub** (rewind a few seconds) and a **freeze frame** that pauses an enemy.
6. **Every run is a unique cut.** The seed means no two players ever see the same film. "There's no definitive version."

## Ending

- **The party is a wrap party.** Clapping, "That's a wrap on [your name]!", crew T-shirts, the stalker with its mask off holding a coffee.
- **The Collector is the Director.** Offer: *"You've earned your director's cut. Hand over the reels, and we'll build you any world you want."*
- **Yes → the theatrical cut:** a bright, polished, happy ending. Too happy.
- **No → the director's cut:** the reels are taken anyway, and you see a few seconds of the uncut, darker version before cut to black.
